ICE has not released detention or deportation statistics since April 2026, blacking out the public record on the scope of mass deportations at the exact moment enforcement is hitting record pace ([[2026-07-23--ice-nears-600k-deportations-july-arrest-record]]). The Marshall Project reports the agency is in a legal dispute over whether the transparency requirement — embedded in an appropriations bill — still applies. The Deportation Data Project (UC Berkeley/UCLA) has filed FOIA requests and sued for the records; the blackout continues as of July 21.

The blackout extends a pattern of deliberately reduced ICE accountability infrastructure the timeline has tracked through 2026, including the elimination of post-release death reporting ([[2026-06-04--venturella-memo-eliminates-post-release-death-reporting-ice-accountability-erasure]]) — official disclosure is being withdrawn from public view in the same period the agency's lethal field incidents and detention scale are both increasing.
