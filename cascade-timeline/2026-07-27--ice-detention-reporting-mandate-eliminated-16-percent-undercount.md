ICE released detention data on July 20 for the first time in more than 90 days, showing 65,765 people in custody — but the release was voluntary: the latest appropriations bill quietly removed the 2018 congressional mandate requiring biweekly updates. Research by Austin Kocher and Adam Sawyer (detentionreports.com) finds ICE's own figures undercount facility populations by up to 16% using a faster "Interval ADP" metric that catches mid-year expansion surges the official averages lag behind. The California City facility illustrates a related ownership maneuver: CoreCivic sold it to DHS after California passed a detention inspection law, leaving the facility federally owned but still privately operated — structurally shielded from state oversight.

This confirms and quantifies the reporting gap the timeline flagged as an open question when the blackout was first documented ([[2026-07-21--ice-data-blackout-since-april-2026]]) — the mandate wasn't merely lapsed or under legal dispute, as ICE's own framing suggested, but was affirmatively removed by an appropriations rider, and even the voluntary data ICE now releases undercounts real-time population by a documented margin.
