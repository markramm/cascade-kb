Following the fatal shootings of Lorenzo Salgado Araujo in Houston (July 7) and Johan Sebastian Duran Guerrero in Biddeford, Maine (July 13) — both during ICE vehicle stops — DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin issued internal guidance around July 14 suspending most traffic-stop arrests. A third death compounded the toll within the same week: a man in Florida was struck and killed by a tractor-trailer while fleeing federal agents on a highway, a death Mother Jones' sourcing characterized as "foreseeable in the most tragic way." Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez called the Houston and Biddeford killings "criminal" and "an unacceptable abuse of power" in a July 14 interview, specifically noting that Guerrero held work authorization and a valid Social Security number — undercutting any "wanted fugitive" framing of the encounter.

The halt did not last a full day. President Trump publicly overruled DHS's pause on July 15, restoring the traffic-stop-arrest practice roughly 24 hours after it began. This is a distinct and separate event from the two individual shootings already documented ([[2026-07-07--ice-kills-lorenzo-salgado-araujo-houston-narrative-collapse]], [[2026-07-13--ice-fatal-shooting-biddeford-maine-weaponize-vehicle]]): it captures the institution's own momentary retreat from a tactic that had killed three people in a week, and the immediate presidential override of that retreat — confirming that arrest-quota pressure takes precedence over the safety concern DHS's own secretary had just acted on. It also extends the "weaponized the vehicle" justification pattern already tracked in [[2026-07-11--eyes-on-ice-documents-vehicle-containment-tactic-civil-enforcement]] and the earlier RAMM piece on the same script's repeated collapse.
