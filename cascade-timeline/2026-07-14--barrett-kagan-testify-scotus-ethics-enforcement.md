Justices Amy Coney Barrett and Elena Kagan appeared before House and Senate Appropriations subcommittees on July 14, where Kagan expressed support for an independent ethics enforcement panel of retired federal judges, while Barrett said she was "less certain." Democrats used the hearing to press for binding ethics enforcement. Rep. Adam Schiff and others subsequently introduced bills extending conflict-of-interest rules to the judiciary and requiring justices to place certain assets in blind trusts.

This is the first instance of a sitting justice publicly endorsing an external, binding ethics-enforcement mechanism, following the documented pattern of undisclosed gifts and travel the timeline has tracked since 2023 ([[2023-12-31--supreme-court-gifts-6-6-million-documented]], [[2024-01-15--supreme-court-systematic-ethics-violations]]) — Kagan's position creates a visible split among the justices on whether the Court should accept externally enforceable rules or continue relying on internal, non-binding guidance.
