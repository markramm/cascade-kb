Trump fired the top four staff of the Election Assistance Commission (EAC) on July 9, 2026, eliminating the agency's senior technical and operational expertise needed to certify voting systems ahead of the 2026 midterms. Reporting from Miles Taylor (former DHS official, publishing as Defiance News) separately describes the administration potentially using the SAVE Act's citizenship-documentation requirements as a "backdoor" for voter-roll purges implemented through OPM and GSA executive action rather than the standard state-federal election-administration process — modeled, per the reporting, on a tactic that purged 340,000 eligible voters in Georgia. The specific backdoor-implementation mechanism is Taylor's interpretive analysis rather than independently confirmed reporting and should be treated as a documented lead pending tier-1 corroboration.

The EAC staff terminations land the same week as DOJ's escalation to criminal threats against state election officials ([[2026-07-07--doj-threatens-criminal-prosecution-election-officials-50-states]]) — together describing a coordinated pressure campaign against every layer of election administration (federal certification staff, state officials, and voter rolls themselves) within the same election cycle.
