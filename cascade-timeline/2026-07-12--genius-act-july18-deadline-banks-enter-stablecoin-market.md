Six federal agencies — OCC, FDIC, NCUA, Treasury, FinCEN, and OFAC — face a statutory July 18, 2026 deadline to finalize GENIUS Act stablecoin rules. Once published, JPMorgan, Bank of America, and other banks that have announced stablecoin readiness can legally issue payment stablecoins for the first time. The same rules govern non-bank issuers including Trump's World Liberty Financial, whose USD1 stablecoin is already the fourth-largest by market cap; OCC's proposed rule sets a $5M capital floor, and FDIC has confirmed stablecoin holders receive no deposit insurance. If any of the six agencies misses the deadline, the statute provides no fallback until January 18, 2027.

The rulemaking reaches its deadline in a structurally compromised posture: independent regulatory-agency removal protections were eliminated in Trump v. Slaughter two weeks earlier ([[2026-06-29--scotus-overturns-humphreys-executor-presidential-firing-power]]), and the Trump family's documented direct financial interest in WLFI now exceeds $14B. This is the deadline arriving at the end of the arc the timeline already tracks through the OCC's industry-tilted rulemaking ([[2026-06-24--genius-act-occ-stablecoin-charters-crypto-industry-reserve-standards]]) and the AML/sanctions comment period closing with WLF conspicuously absent from the public record ([[2026-06-09--genius-act-aml-comment-period-closes-wlf-verified-absent-july-18-framework-effective]]).
