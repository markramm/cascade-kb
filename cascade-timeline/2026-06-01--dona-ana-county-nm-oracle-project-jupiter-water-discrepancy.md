As of June 2026, Dona Ana County, New Mexico is questioning Oracle's Project Jupiter — a 1,400-acre AI data-center campus in Santa Teresa approved via a $165 billion industrial revenue bond in September 2025 — after Oracle publicly claimed the facility would use 20,000 gallons of water per day while state engineers confirmed usage could run up to 1 million gallons per day: a 50-times discrepancy in a water-scarce desert region. Two resident lawsuits allege Open Meetings Act violations, permit fraud, and conflicts of interest in the approval process. A community opposition group is actively organizing, and a permit hearing drew intense public pushback.

The discrepancy is a specific, quantified instance of the data-center resource-capture pattern spreading across counties nationally, alongside moratorium and pushback fights in DeSoto County FL ([[2026-06-25--desoto-county-fl-data-center-moratorium-dcip-carve-out]]), Imperial County CA ([[2026-06-16--imperial-county-ca-reverses-data-center-approval-moratorium]]), and New York State ([[2026-06-03--new-york-legislature-passes-statewide-data-center-moratorium-s10642]]), and connects to Oracle's separate ratepayer-cost-shift fight in Wisconsin ([[2026-06-19--oracle-sues-wisconsin-psc-data-center-ratepayer-cost-shift]]). It is a concrete water-use data point for the broader fiscal-capture pattern tracked at [[2026-06-11--data-center-fiscal-capture-tax-exemptions-state-budgets-electoral-salience]].
