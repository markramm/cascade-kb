The ACLU and Protect Democracy filed suit July 15, 2026 to block a completed DOJ national voter surveillance and purge database — the first federal consolidation of confidential voter registration data from all 50 states and DC into a single record system, now cross-referenced against SAVE, an immigration-verification database with a documented history of false positives. The lawsuit argues the cross-reference creates substantial risk of mass wrongful purges of eligible voters. The threat is time-critical: the National Voter Registration Act's 90-day blackout on systematic roll purges before a federal election begins August 5, 2026, ahead of the November midterms — while Republicans are simultaneously pushing the Supreme Court to gut that same blackout protection via an Arizona case set for the Court's October term.

This is the completion and legal challenge stage of the database-building campaign the timeline first documented in September 2025 ([[2025-09-09--doj-demands-voter-data-centralized-database]]), when DOJ began demanding voter files from more than 30 states. Ten months of state-by-state data collection and litigation (including DOJ's subsequent suits against holdout states) have produced the consolidated system now facing its first direct legal challenge — landing in the same week as DOJ's escalation to criminal threats against election officials ([[2026-07-07--doj-threatens-criminal-prosecution-election-officials-50-states]]) and Trump's EAC commissioner firings ([[2026-07-10--trump-fires-eac-commissioners-hicks-hovland-after-humphreys-executor]]), describing a coordinated pressure campaign against every layer of election administration ahead of a single midterm cycle.
