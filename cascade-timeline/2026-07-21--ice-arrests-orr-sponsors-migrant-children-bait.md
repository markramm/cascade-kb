ICE is systematically arresting relatives who complete months of Office of Refugee Resettlement vetting to sponsor unaccompanied migrant children in federal shelters — after the Trump administration removed the rule barring ORR from sharing sponsor data with ICE. Attorneys describe the children as being used as bait: one Honduran woman spent nearly a year on government-mandated vetting before being arrested at her apartment after coming forward. Children are now stranded in shelters as prospective sponsors, aware of the arrest risk, decline to come forward.

The policy converts a child-welfare vetting process into an enforcement funnel, extending the pattern of cross-agency data-sharing weaponization the timeline has tracked in Medicaid-to-ICE ([[2026-07-17--ice-medicaid-palantir-illegal-data-transfer]]) and other federal-benefits-to-enforcement pipelines through 2026 — sponsors who followed the government's own required process to reunify a child are the ones targeted.
