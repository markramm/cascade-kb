An ICE agent fatally shot Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, a 52-year-old Mexican national who had lived in the U.S. for more than 30 years and was in the process of obtaining legal status, as he drove his construction crew to a Houston job site on July 7, 2026. ICE's initial account claimed agents fired in self-defense after Salgado Araujo rammed a law-enforcement vehicle and ignored verbal commands during a "targeted operation" traffic stop. That account has since collapsed: the three men riding in his work van told an attorney no ramming occurred and at no point were agents' lives in danger, and the Harris County Medical Examiner ruled the death a homicide from a penetrating gunshot wound. DHS acknowledged the involved officers had not yet received body-worn cameras, attributing the gap to shutdown-related funding delays. Houston Mayor John Whitmire, who initially claimed the city had no jurisdiction to investigate, reversed course as the case became a local flashpoint.

This is the 10th person killed by ICE since January 2025, and follows the pattern of contested lethal-force incidents and post-hoc narrative reversal already documented at Camp East Montana ([[2026-07-03--propublica-camp-east-montana-mental-health-crisis-death]]) and in the HRW/Reuters detention-death-rate reporting ([[2026-06-17--reuters-ice-detention-death-rate-doubles-50-deaths-overcrowding-medical-failure]]). Note: the ledger digest that surfaced this story incorrectly characterized Salgado Araujo as a U.S. citizen; multiple tier-1 outlets (ABC13, Washington Post, CNN, Houston Public Media) confirm he was a longtime Mexican-national resident pursuing legal status — the "wrong man" framing refers to the collapsed self-defense narrative and mistaken-identity target, not citizenship status.
