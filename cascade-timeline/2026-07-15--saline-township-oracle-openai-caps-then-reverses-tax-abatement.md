Saline Township (Washtenaw County, MI, near Ann Arbor) approved a 12-year, 50% tax abatement on July 15, 2026 for "The Barn" — a 250-acre Oracle/OpenAI Stargate-affiliated AI campus developed by The Related Companies — but capped the abatement basis at the project's original $4.8 billion valuation rather than its current $43.1 billion stated valuation, cutting the annual subsidy roughly 9x (from an estimated $147M to under $20M) and adding a clawback provision recoverable if the project is never completed. The approval itself was compelled by a prior court order from an earlier Related lawsuit, but the township used the discretion it had to constrain the dollar amount.

The constraint lasted 72 hours. The board called a special meeting July 18 and voted 5-0 to reverse itself, approving the full exemption on the $43.1 billion figure after attorney David Landry warned that the underlying consent judgment "does not say the township may approve it, it says that it shall approve it," and that residents could face special assessments to cover litigation costs if Oracle sued over the reduced figure. The audience booed. This is the consent-judgment-as-coercion mechanism operating in real time: a legal agreement reached outside any public vote binding a township to grant the maximum benefit a hyperscaler requests, with the deliberative body's only real choice being how much it costs residents to resist before capitulating.

This is a distinct and more explicit capture mechanism than the standard moratorium-vote pattern already tracked across dozens of counties — most jurisdictions in the datacenter-resistance wave (see [[2026-07-22--montgomery-co-md-datacenter-18month-moratorium-vote]]) are exercising real discretion; Saline Township's board had none once the underlying consent judgment existed, and its attempt to use the discretion it did have collapsed within three days under the threat of litigation costs being passed to residents.
