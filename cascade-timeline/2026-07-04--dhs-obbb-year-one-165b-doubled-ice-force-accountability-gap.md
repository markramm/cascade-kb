DHS published a year-one accounting of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's enforcement buildout on July 4, 2026, documenting $165B in appropriations since the bill's July 4, 2025 signing. ICE's agent corps doubled from roughly 10,000 to 22,000; 80,000 new detention beds came online; the 287(g) state-and-local partnership program is fully funded; and ICE/Border Patrol officers received $10,000 annual bonuses. The DHS release does not address that detention expansion was partially funded through ITAR/military-procurement authorities that bypass competitive bidding and standard inspector-general review triggers — a claim from Brennan Center analysis not yet independently confirmed in tier-1 reporting, but consistent with the WEXMAC contracting-vehicle pattern already documented across the warehouse-conversion pipeline. Brennan Center characterizes the result as a "deportation-industrial complex" whose oversight architecture has not kept pace with its enforcement buildout.

This closes out the first year of the funding mechanism the timeline tracks from its original passage ([[2025-07-04--obbba-ice-detention-appropriations-45-billion-fy2029-funding-mechanism]]) through the mid-year enforcement bill ([[2026-06-10--trump-signs-70-billion-ice-cbp-enforcement-bill-anti-weaponization-fund-survives]]) — the 100,000-bed target is now reported roughly 80% funded, operationalizing at scale the accountability gap already visible in the Camp East Montana GAO waste finding ([[2026-06-19--ice-megajail-program-retreats-gao-finds-camp-east-montana-wasted-millions]]).
