ProPublica reports that in its October 2025 term, the Supreme Court for the first time decided more cases through secret-ballot, unsigned "shadow docket" orders with minimal or no written justification than through argued cases with signed opinions — a structural milestone in the shadow docket's shift from an emergency-relief mechanism to the primary vehicle for sweeping, functionally unreviewable executive-branch wins. The largest single category of shadow-docket grants in the term was stays of lower-court injunctions blocking deportations and other immigration-enforcement actions, directly benefiting the administration's removal agenda.

This milestone is the aggregate, term-level confirmation of a pattern this timeline has tracked case-by-case: the string of shadow-docket immigration stays including Noem v. Perdomo ([[2025-09-08--scotus-stays-noem-v-perdomo-la-immigration-enforcement]]), Noem v. Doe / CHNV parole termination ([[2025-05-30--scotus-stays-noem-v-doe-chnv-parole-termination]]), Noem v. NTPSA / Venezuelan TPS termination ([[2025-05-19--scotus-stays-noem-v-ntpsa-venezuelan-tps-termination]]), and the Court's rejection of Trump's bid to silence immigration judges ([[2025-12-19--scotus-rejects-trump-bid-to-silence-immigration-judges]]). It also connects to the broader emergency/shadow-docket expansion of executive power already flagged in this timeline ([[2025-10-03--supreme-court-emergency-docket-expands-trump-power]]) and the NYT's shadow-docket leak reporting on internal deliberations ([[2026-04-18--kantor-liptak-nyt-shadow-docket-leak-clean-power-plan-memos]]). Where those entries document individual rulings, this one names the term-level structural shift ProPublica quantifies: the shadow docket is no longer the exception to the Court's process, it is now the plurality mode of decision.
